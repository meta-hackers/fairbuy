# Alternatives to FairBuy

## Name the judgment, not the transaction

FairBuy sounds like a promise about ethical commerce or fair prices. This product does something more particular: it asks what matters, researches the options, explains compromises, and learns from “not that one.” The name should leave room for that.

These proposals deliberately avoid shopping-assistant naming formulas: no *Shop + noun*, no *-ify*, no *-ly*, and no AI badge. The goal is an unexpected but explainable name—not an unusual spelling of “buy.” I cannot guarantee another AI would never suggest them.

**These are creative proposals, not cleared brands.** The multilingual expansion below includes preliminary web research into meanings and selected existing uses, not comprehensive linguistic or trademark clearance. No proposed domain is confirmed available to register. Familiar phrases can also be harder to own in search.

## Strongest directions

### 1. The Other Pocket

*For everything the price tag leaves out.*

One pocket holds the budget; the other holds the reasons: whether it fits your desk, survives your commute, or works with what you already own. A slightly mysterious, physical name for a product that weighs more than money.

- **Why it fits:** accommodates research, preferences, and trade-offs without sounding like a discount service.
- **Watch out:** could initially suggest a wallet or personal-finance app. Pair it with “Shopping research for what matters to you.”

### 2. The Almost Pile

*Turn “almost right” into “that's the one.”*

The pile of things you nearly chose is useful evidence. This name makes the product's feedback loop—not a supposedly magical first recommendation—the central idea.

- **Why it fits:** distinctive, conversational, and unusually honest about how decisions happen.
- **Watch out:** “almost” can imply the product never quite succeeds. The supporting copy must complete the thought.

### 3. On These Terms

*Find it. On your terms.*

Your requirements are not decorative filters. They are the terms under which a purchase makes sense. Calm, assertive, and broad enough to outgrow a shopping interface.

- **Why it fits:** puts the user's constraints ahead of the seller's pitch.
- **Watch out:** has a legal or negotiation flavour; avoid contract-like visual design.

### 4. Not the Blue One

*Every “no” gets you closer.*

A name lifted from the kind of ordinary correction that makes a recommendation useful. It suggests an assistant that listens rather than one that announces a winner.

- **Why it fits:** memorable, playful, and directly connected to rejecting products and refining the brief.
- **Watch out:** long, and sounds colour-specific without context. Better for a characterful consumer brand than a formal procurement tool.

### 5. A Conti Fatti

*Beyond the price. All things considered.*

An Italian expression roughly meaning “when all is said and done” or “when you do the sums.” It captures the moment when price, practical needs, and compromises become one decision.

- **Why it fits:** grounded in everyday speech rather than startup vocabulary; especially worth considering for an Italy-first launch.
- **Watch out:** pronunciation and meaning need explanation outside Italian-speaking markets; it may also suggest accounting.

### 6. The Spare Chair

*A second perspective before you buy.*

An extra place at the table for someone who asks the question you forgot to ask. Human and hospitable without pretending the assistant is a person.

- **Why it fits:** positions the product as an adviser, not a storefront or checkout agent.
- **Watch out:** strong furniture associations. Use broad product examples immediately.

## More unusual routes

| **Pocket Footnote** | The small detail that saves you from the wrong purchase. | Could sound like a notes app. |
| **The Unbought** | A research space where buying is not the default outcome. | More editorial and anti-consumption than mainstream retail. |

| **A Smaller Maybe** | Research that narrows uncertainty rather than manufacturing certainty. | Poetic, but not immediately commercial. |
| **Measure Twice Club** | A deliberate-purchase habit, borrowed from the workshop rather than retail. | Long; “club” may imply membership. |

## First-round recommendation

**The Other Pocket** is the best balance of personality, warmth, and room to grow. **The Almost Pile** is the most faithful to the actual interaction. **Not the Blue One** is the boldest choice if memorability matters more than immediate category clarity.

Before choosing, say each finalist aloud in these sentences: “I found it through ___,” “Ask ___ before you buy,” and “___ helped me rule that one out.” Then test whether people remember the name—and understand the product—without seeing the explanation above.

## Expanded direction: names from other languages

Trovato is a useful new benchmark: a real word that names the outcome, not the technology. The most interesting alternatives name **recognition, suitability, or considered judgment**. Translations below describe relevant senses, not every meaning of a word. Have native speakers review the finalists in context.

Domain spellings in backticks are **creative pairings, not availability claims**. A search result that does not show a domain is not evidence that the domain is unregistered.

| Name | Language and relevant meaning | Domain idea | Why consider it / reservation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Trovato** | Italian: “found.” | `trovato.top`, `trovato.fyi` | Warm, pronounceable, outcome-led. `.fyi` emphasises informed decisions; `.top` suggests rankings. Also a surname and ordinary word, so distinctiveness needs checking. |
| **Eccolo** | Italian: “here it is” / “there it is,” referring to a masculine singular thing. | `eccolo.li` | A small moment of discovery. With the extension, it evokes *eccolo lì*: “there it is.” Not a gender-neutral literal translation of “found it.” |
| **Azzecca** | Italian: a form of *azzeccare*, “get right” / “hit upon.” | `azzecca.la` | The whole address reads *azzeccala*: roughly “get it right,” with a feminine object such as *la scelta* (“the choice”) understood. Strong Italy-first personality; spelling is harder internationally. |
| **Calza** | Italian: “fits” as a verb form; also “sock” or “stocking” as a noun. | `calza.fyi` | Exceptionally compact and relevant to fit. The clothing association is substantial, not a minor footnote. |
| **Genau So** | German: “exactly like that.” | `genau.so` | The domain completes a real expression. Sounds like the user finally recognising what they meant. Pronunciation needs teaching outside German-speaking markets. |
| **Klopt** | Dutch: “is correct” / “checks out” in the relevant sense of *kloppen*. | `klopt.fyi` | Short, percussive, and centred on judgment. Can also refer to knocking or beating; the final consonant cluster is awkward for some speakers. |
| **Sopiva** | Finnish: “suitable” / “appropriate.” | `sopiva.guide` | Almost exactly the product promise, with a gentle sound. **Downgrade:** an existing Sopiva job-matching service already occupies nearby “find what fits” territory. |
| **Osuva** | Finnish: “apt,” “well-chosen,” or “on-target,” depending on context. | `osuva.fyi` | A more distinctive sound than generic “smart choice” branding. **Downgrade:** existing Osuva technology businesses and other uses surfaced. |
| **Pile Poil** | French colloquial expression: “spot on” / “exactly.” | `pilepoil.fyi` | Playful and tactile rather than technological. Pronounced approximately “peel pwal”; spelling and pronunciation will diverge for English speakers. |
| **Encaja** | Spanish: “fits,” from *encajar*. | `encaja.fyi` | Makes compatibility and personal fit the central idea. The Spanish *j* is not pronounced like English *j*; could sound like a fitting or interiors service. |
| **Achei** | Portuguese: “I found”; can also mean “I thought,” depending on context. | `achei.fyi` | A short exclamation of discovery, approximately “ah-SHAY.” **Downgrade:** an existing Achei app and other commercial uses surfaced. |
| **Pittari** | Japanese, ぴったり: “exactly,” “precisely,” or fitting closely/perfectly in context. | `pittari.to` | *Pittari to* is an actual Japanese adverbial construction, so the extension has a linguistic role. **Downgrade:** PITTARI already appears in a Shopify size-chart app and clothing brands. |
| **Cocok** | Indonesian: “suitable” / “compatible” / “matching.” | `cocok.fyi` | Visually distinctive and very relevant. Pronounced approximately “CHO-chok,” not “ko-kok”; test English-language misreadings before pursuing it. |

### My strongest multilingual candidates

- **Eccolo** — more expressive than Trovato: the moment of pointing to the right thing.
- **Genau So** — the strongest pairing of phrase and domain, especially for a German-speaking audience.
- **Encaja** — a clear fit-led direction for Spanish-speaking markets.
- **Pile Poil** — the most playful French direction, with a real pronunciation trade-off.
- **Trovato** — still the strongest broad-audience baseline of this set in my judgment.

The less familiar words are not automatically more ownable. Sopiva and Pittari are good examples of attractive meanings that already appeal to other product builders.

## Names made better by the domain

Three approaches are worth separating: a domain can **complete a phrase**, **complete a word**, or **explain the product's role**. A merely unusual extension is not enough.

| Brand / spoken name | Domain concept | What the dot contributes | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Genau So** | `genau.so` | Completes “exactly like that” in German. | Best understood by German speakers; `.so` is Somalia's country-code domain. |
| **Eccolo Lì** | `eccolo.li` | Completes the Italian expression “there it is.” | The accent in *lì* is omitted in the domain; `.li` is Liechtenstein's country-code domain. |
| **Azzeccala** | `azzecca.la` | Splits a real Italian imperative: “get it right,” with a feminine object understood. | More local and harder to spell; `.la` is Laos's country-code domain. |
| **Soppesare** | `soppesa.re` | Reassembles the Italian verb “to weigh carefully,” literally or figuratively. | A particularly apt, less obvious metaphor, but a long spoken name. `.re` is Réunion's country-code domain. |
| **Ponderare** | `pondera.re` | Reassembles the Italian verb “to ponder” / “weigh up.” | More internationally recognisable than Soppesare, but less surprising. Same registry considerations as `.re` above. |
| **Mull It** | `mull.it` | Reads as the English invitation to think it over. | Nice counterpoint to impulse shopping; *mull* is not universally familiar. `.it` has registrant eligibility rules. |
| **Narrow It** | `narrow.it` | Turns the address into the product's action: reduce the options. | Very clear, less emotionally distinctive. Same `.it` eligibility issue. |
| **Settle On It** | `settleon.it` | Completes an English phrase about reaching a decision. | Longer, and “settle” can imply accepting second best. |
| **This or That** | `thisorth.at` | The extension supplies the final two letters of *that*. | A clever comparison brand, but awkward to dictate: “thisorth dot at.” `.at` is Austria's country-code domain. |
| **Trovato** | `trovato.fyi` | Frames the service as research and evidence rather than a seller. | Less triumphant than `.top`, but more faithful to the actual product. The extension explains rather than completes the name. |

### What I would not pursue

- **`passt.so`** is a neat German phrase-domain pairing, but a live site already surfaced at that exact address. It is not an available-name recommendation.
- **Pittari as the lead brand:** a shopping-software collision is too close to ignore, even though `pittari.to` is linguistically appealing.
- **A split you must explain every time:** if people remember the word but cannot reconstruct the address, the domain trick is working against you. `thisorth.at` is the riskiest example here.
- **A subdomain mistaken for a registration:** `sleep.on.it` would require control of `on.it` or permission from its owner. It is not a normal standalone second-level domain purchase.

## Revised shortlist

| Priority | Candidate | Why it earns a place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Trovato** — `trovato.top` or `trovato.fyi` | Best balance of warmth, simple meaning, and a brand that can stand without its extension. Keep the user's original candidate in contention. |
| 2 | **Eccolo Lì** — `eccolo.li` | My favourite expressive Italian phrase-domain pairing: “there it is.” |
| 3 | **Genau So** — `genau.so` | My favourite German pairing: the address itself delivers the recognition moment. |
| 4 | **Soppesare** — `soppesa.re` | The least startup-like direction: careful judgment rather than shopping excitement. |
| 5 | **Mull It** — `mull.it` | Strong English-language positioning for deliberate purchases rather than impulse buying. |

**My call:** Trovato remains the safer creative choice; Eccolo Lì is the more characterful alternative. Soppesare is the unusual one I would test rather than dismiss—it describes the work this product actually does.

Before committing, check registration and renewal prices (including premium pricing), registrant eligibility, trademark conflicts in target markets, existing apps, and native-speaker reactions. Test the spoken name and the typed address separately. Do not rename the product solely because an attractive domain happens to be cheap.

## Third round: 30 more names

This round pushes further into spoken expressions, quiet confidence, and names that sound unlike shopping software. **All domain suggestions remain unchecked for registration availability and price.** Except for the selected meaning references below, this is a creative expansion, not an existing-business search. A name being new to this document does not mean it is unused elsewhere.

These lean into the product's independence from checkout. Some are intentionally better as a distinctive consumer brand than as a conventional company name.

### Five from this batch worth testing first

1. **Me Cuadra** — the most natural expression of “this makes sense for me,” rather than “the algorithm says this is best.”
2. **Op Maat / `opma.at`** — one of the neatest domain splits, with a meaning that matches personalised requirements.
3. **Worth Your Space / `worthyour.space`** — the strongest English emotional positioning: buy something that deserves to be there.
4. **A Pennello / `apennello.fit`** — warm, expressive, and more physical than a conventional technology name.
5. **Nothing Urgent / `nothingurgent.today`** — the bold outsider. A recognisable point of view in a market full of pressure to buy now.

**Most practical addition:** Me Cuadra for a Spanish-speaking audience; Worth Your Space for an English-speaking one. **Most interesting domain-led addition:** Op Maat. **Most distinctive brand attitude:** Nothing Urgent.

These do not replace the earlier shortlist automatically. Trovato is easier to carry across markets than many of the more idiomatic options; the new names trade that simplicity for character.
